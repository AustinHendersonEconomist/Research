The question

New Zealand's transport agency values a statistical life at NZ$12.5 million. Spread over a 40-year-old's discounted, quality-adjusted remaining life, that is about NZ$516,000 per QALY. The Treasury values a QALY at about NZ$36,000. The paper asks what theory says each number measures, and splits the fourteen-fold gap into parts that can each be measured.

Proposition 1 and Remark 1

When a statistical life is a stack of QALYs

Proposition 1 (common value)
Suppose (L) utility at reference consumption is proportional to quality of life, u(c̄, h) = Q(h) u(c̄, h*), and (M) the marginal utility of consumption does not depend on health. Then VSLa = v* · DQALEa + NCa, and willingness to pay for better health is v* per discounted QALY. ∎

Each future year enters the VSL with a weight. Under L and M that weight is quality of life, Q. Under the popular multiplicative form u = Q(h) w(c), condition M fails. People then shift consumption toward healthy years, and each year is weighted by Q1/η instead, where η is the curvature of utility.

Dividing a VSL by DQALE therefore assumes M as well as L. This is the restriction that Bleichrodt and Quiggin's equivalence quietly needs.

Propositions 2 and 3

Why the bias only runs one way

Proposition 2 (one-signed bias)
If L holds but the marginal utility of consumption depends on health, then with optimal consumption ν(t) ≥ v* Q(h(t)) + y(t) − c̄, with equality only if consumption does not move. So the VSL can only exceed its QALY-consistent benchmark. ∎
Proposition 3 (it is second-order)
If consumption moves by a fraction δ, the excess is (η/2) δ² c̄: about 1% of a healthy year's value for δ = 0.2, η = 1.5 and v*/c̄ = 3. ∎

The proof is the picture on the right. A concave utility curve always lies below its tangent lines. When health changes the marginal utility of money, the person moves along the curve to a new consumption level ct. The tangent at ct passes above the curve at c̄, and that overshoot is exactly how much the life-year is worth beyond its QALY benchmark. Moving left or right makes no difference: the gap is never negative, and it shrinks with the square of the distance.

Proposition 4

Two kinds of context premium, two age profiles

Proposition 4 (context premia)
πk,a = ωk (1 + αk Da,T / (μ VSL0a)). Misperception ω is flat in age. The anxiety premium grows with age as remaining healthy life shrinks. ∎

People pay more to avoid dreaded or involuntary deaths. Part of that premium is anxiety felt while exposed. Anxiety is a real welfare cost and belongs in appraisal. Part is misperception: a vivid risk feels larger than it is. A planner should correct a misperception, not pay for it.

The two parts are separable in data because they behave differently with age. Anxiety is felt for as long as the exposure lasts, whatever one's age, so it is a larger share of a smaller remaining life for older people.

Proposition 5

How scope insensitivity inflates a VSL

Proposition 5 (measurement factor)
If stated willingness to pay is β Δpθ, a VSL measured with risk changes of size Δps and applied to policies of size Δp0 is inflated by M = (Δps/Δp0)θ−1. ∎

Theory says willingness to pay for a tiny risk reduction should be proportional to its size: twice the risk reduction, twice the money. Survey answers are often much flatter than that. Dividing a flat answer by a very small risk gives a very large VSL. Testing two sample groups with different risk sizes reveals θ, and the inflation can then be removed.

Demonstration · stated WTP against risk reduction

Proposition 6

A threshold is v* divided by a shadow price

Proposition 6 (decision rule)
Fund project j in sector s if v* Σ π̃k ΔQjk + ΔYj ≥ λs Cj. For pure health projects this is a cost-per-QALY threshold ks = v*/λs. Efficiency requires every λs to equal the marginal cost of public funds. ∎

A low health threshold does not mean health is worth less in hospitals than on roads. It means the health budget is tight: each health dollar has a high shadow price λ. Moving a dollar from a sector with a low λ to one with a high λ raises welfare by the difference.

Demonstration · same v*, different budgets
